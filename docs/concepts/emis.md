# What is an EMIS

An education management information system (EMIS) is how an education
authority knows what is happening in its education system: how many
learners there are and where, whether they attend and learn, who teaches
them, and what it all costs.

UNESCO defines it as:

> a system for the collection, integration, processing, maintenance and
> dissemination of data and information to support decision-making,
> policy-analysis and formulation, planning, monitoring and management at
> all levels of an education system.
>
> — UNESCO, [*Education for All by 2015: Will we make it?*](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000154743),
> EFA Global Monitoring Report 2008, p. 101

The same definition goes on to say that an EMIS is "a system of people,
technology, models, methods, processes, procedures, rules and
regulations". That matters: an EMIS is not one piece of software. It is
the arrangement by which data about education is collected, trusted and
used.

## Who relies on it

| Who | What they need from it |
| --- | --- |
| Ministry planners and leaders | How many places, teachers and how much money are needed, and where |
| District and regional officers | Which institutions need support, and whether returns are complete |
| Institution leaders and teachers | Their own learners: enrolment, attendance, results, support needs |
| Learners and guardians | Their own records, and correct ones |
| Statisticians and researchers | Consistent figures over time and between places |
| The public and international bodies | Published statistics, such as the indicators for the Sustainable Development Goal on education |

## Two kinds of work

An EMIS does two different jobs, and much of this blueprint follows from
keeping them apart.

- **Recording.** Day to day, it keeps records about individual people
  and institutions: this learner is enrolled here, attended today, sat
  this examination. These records change all the time and must be
  correct for each person.
- **Counting.** Periodically, it produces official figures: enrolment by
  grade and district on census day, the pupil–teacher ratio, the
  completion rate. These are counted *as at a reference date*, published,
  and then not changed silently, because policy and funding depend on
  them.

A good EMIS lets the second be derived from the first, so the figures can
be traced back to the records they count.

## What this blueprint covers

This blueprint describes the **data platform** behind an EMIS: where the
data lives, how it moves, how it is governed, who may see it, and how
figures are defined and published. It does not prescribe the
applications people type into, such as a school management system or a
census form. Those are *products* that run on the platform.
