# What is an EMIS

An education management information system (EMIS) is how an education
authority knows what's happening in its education system. It tells the
authority how many learners there are and where they are, whether they
attend and whether they learn, who teaches them, and what it all costs.

UNESCO defines an EMIS as:

> a system for the collection, integration, processing, maintenance and
> dissemination of data and information to support decision-making,
> policy-analysis and formulation, planning, monitoring and management at
> all levels of an education system.
>
> — UNESCO, [*Education for All by 2015: Will we make it?*](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000154743),
> EFA Global Monitoring Report 2008, p. 101

The same definition goes on to say that an EMIS is "a system of people,
technology, models, methods, processes, procedures, rules and
regulations". That matters, because it means an EMIS isn't one piece of
software. It's the whole arrangement through which an authority collects
data about education, comes to trust it, and uses it.

## Who relies on it

| Who | What they need from it |
| --- | --- |
| Ministry planners and leaders | How many places, teachers and how much money are needed, and where |
| District and regional officers | Which institutions need support, and whether their returns are complete |
| Institution leaders and teachers | The enrolment, attendance, results and support needs of their own learners |
| Learners and guardians | Their own records, and for those records to be correct |
| Statisticians and researchers | Consistent figures over time and between places |
| The public and international bodies | Published statistics, such as the indicators for the Sustainable Development Goal on education |

## Two kinds of work

An EMIS does two different jobs, and much of this blueprint comes from
keeping them apart.

The first job is **recording**. Day to day, an EMIS keeps records about
individual people and institutions: for example, that a learner is
enrolled here, attended today and sat this examination. These records
change all the time, and they have to be correct for each person.

The second job is **counting**. From time to time, an EMIS produces
official figures, such as enrolment by grade and district on census day,
the pupil–teacher ratio, or the completion rate. We count these *as at a
reference date* and publish them, and after that nobody changes them
quietly, because policy and funding depend on them.

A good EMIS derives the counts from the records, so that you can trace
any figure back to the records it counts.

## What this blueprint covers

This blueprint describes three things: the **domain** an EMIS records, the
**rules** for handling its data, and the governed **data platform** that
holds it. That covers where the data lives, how it moves, how it's
governed, who may see it, and how figures are defined and published. It
doesn't prescribe the applications that people type into, such as a
school management system or a census form. Those are *products*, and
they run on the platform.
