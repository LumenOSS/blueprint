## Summary

Divide the EMIS domain into ten bounded contexts, each with its own
language, steward and catalogs, and design them with domain-driven design:
subdomain types, a context map, aggregates with their invariants, and
domain events. Two clauses make the boundaries enforceable: contexts
integrate only through published interfaces, and they publish domain
events in a catalogue.

## Motivation

The domain model had one language file for the whole domain and a list
of catalogs, but it did not say which parts matter most, where one model
ends and the next begins, what each record guarantees, or how parts
communicate. Without that:

- the same word drifts between meanings: a "programme" of study and an
  entitlement "programme" of free meals were one term;
- products are tempted to read each other's operational databases, which
  couples their release cycles and leaks data across stewards;
- rules such as "a learner has one main enrolment at a time" are implied
  by the data model but never stated, so implementations disagree.

## Specification

- **Contexts.** Reference (shared kernel), People, Institutions, Learning,
  Workforce, Curriculum, Assessment, Statistics, Records and Operations.
  The [context map](../../CONTEXT-MAP.md) lists them and their
  relationships; each has a `CONTEXT.md` under `docs/domain/contexts/`,
  replacing the single root `CONTEXT.md`.
- **Design.** [Domain design](../domain/design.md) classifies the contexts
  as core (Learning, Statistics), supporting or generic; draws the context
  map; lists each context's aggregates with their invariants, and its
  domain events; and maps contexts onto catalogs. Two catalogs are added
  to the domain model: `statistics` and `records`.
- **Terms.** "Entitlement scheme" replaces "programme" for benefits such
  as meals or transport, and the `programmes` catalog is renamed
  `entitlements`; "programme" means a course of education only.
- **Clauses.**
  - CAP-INTEROPERABILITY.8 (MUST): bounded contexts integrate only through
    published interfaces (registry interfaces, domain events, and gold
    tables with their semantic-layer definitions); no product reads or
    writes another context's operational database.
  - CAP-INTEROPERABILITY.9 (SHOULD): each context publishes its domain
    events in a catalogue, named in the past tense in its language, and
    other contexts react to events instead of polling records.

## Rationale

- **Ten contexts rather than one per catalog.** Catalogs are units of
  storage and access; contexts are units of language and ownership.
  Finance, resources, entitlement schemes and safety share one steward and
  a generic character, so they form one Operations context with four
  catalogs.
- **Statistics as core.** Official figures are why an authority runs an
  EMIS, and their rules (reference dates, attestation, locking) differ
  from those of the operational records they count.
- **Records as its own context.** Retention, disposal and disclosure are
  policies every context conforms to; giving them one owner avoids ten
  different readings of the same law.
- **Rejected: one shared model.** A single model across all contexts is
  simpler to draw but forces every steward to agree on every term, which
  is the drift this LIP removes.

## Compatibility

Catalogue 0.4.0, a minor release: before 1.0.0, new MUST clauses are
allowed in a minor version
([ADR-0008](../adr/0008-model-the-emis-domain.md)). Both reference bindings list
the two clauses as `planned`. Implementations that already separate
operational databases by product meet CAP-INTEROPERABILITY.8 by showing
their grants.

## Path to active

- **Acceptance criteria:** catalogue 0.4.0 is released with the two
  clauses; every term previously in the root `CONTEXT.md` is in exactly
  one context; both reference bindings give the clauses a status.
- **Implementation plan:** the maintainers make the change in one release;
  the implementation repositories move the clauses to `met` when their
  grants and event catalogues exist.

## Privacy and safeguarding

Positive. Contexts that cannot read each other's operational databases
cannot leak special-category data across stewards by accident, and
domain events carry identifiers and facts, not copies of the person.

## Security

A cross-context connection is now a violation that a grant test detects,
which narrows what a compromised product can reach.
